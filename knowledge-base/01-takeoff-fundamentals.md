# 01 — Takeoff Fundamentals

A **takeoff** (quantity takeoff / material takeoff) is the itemized list of every quantity of work
required to perform a scope on a bid — measured from the plans, governed by the specs. It is the
foundation of the estimate: the estimate prices what the takeoff counts. A takeoff that misses an
item means the bid absorbs that cost for free.

For a flooring subcontractor doing polished concrete and epoxy, a complete takeoff answers:

1. **What system(s)?** — exact finish system per area (exposure class + gloss for polish; system
   build + mil thickness for epoxy)
2. **How much of each?** — SF of floor by system, LF of linear items, EA of countable items
3. **What condition is the slab in, and what does it take to get it ready?** — prep, repair,
   moisture
4. **What surrounds the work?** — protection, phasing, mobilizations, access, testing, mock-ups
5. **What are we NOT doing?** — explicit exclusions, so scope gaps are priced by someone

## Units used on these takeoffs

| Unit | Used for |
|---|---|
| SF (square feet) | Floor area by system, prep, moisture mitigation, protection |
| LF (linear feet) | Cove base, joint fill/sealant, crack repair, saw cuts, edge/hand-grind zones, terminations, trench drains |
| EA (each) | Floor drains, penetrations/column blockouts, mock-ups, mobilizations, moisture test locations, doorway transitions |
| GAL / KIT / UNIT | Material check: densifier, primer, body coats, topcoats, aggregate bags |
| HR / DAY | Standby, phased night work, supervision if spec-driven |
| LS (lump sum) | Submittals, closeout, small fixed-cost items |

## The takeoff process (order matters)

1. **Document inventory** — list every sheet and spec section received; note the addendum level.
   Bidding from an outdated set is the classic fatal error.
2. **Spec read** — the spec defines the system, standards, and quality burden. Read our sections
   word-for-word; skim Division 01 and related sections (see doc 05).
3. **Plan read** — finish plans, finish schedule/legend, room schedule, details, demolition plans
   (existing coatings to remove?), site logistics plan.
4. **Measure** — area-by-area quantities with sheet references (doc 06 has the rules).
5. **Reconcile** — plan quantities vs. finish schedule room list vs. spec area descriptions. Any
   mismatch is an RFI.
6. **Conditions pass** — everything that isn't floor area: prep, repair allowances, moisture,
   protection, phasing, testing, mock-ups.
7. **Scope letter** — written inclusions, exclusions, clarifications, and RFIs.

## Waste and loss factors (defaults, override when spec/site dictates)

| Item | Typical factor |
|---|---|
| Epoxy resin materials | 5–10% over theoretical coverage (porosity, texture, transfer loss) |
| Broadcast aggregate (quartz/sand) | Broadcast to excess ≈ 2× what stays; recover ~50% for reuse; carry 10–15% net waste |
| Flake/chip | 15–20% over theoretical (broadcast-to-rejection loses more) |
| Densifier | +10–20% on porous/rough slabs |
| Joint filler | +10% (joint width variance, spillage) |
| Diamond tooling | Consumption scales with slab hardness and prep condition — hard-troweled burnished slabs eat metal-bonds slower per SF but need more grits |

## The three quantity killers

1. **The slab you can't see.** Existing buildings: unknown coatings, curing compounds, gypcrete
   patches, oil saturation. New construction: cure method, hard-trowel burn, flatness. Always carry
   a prep qualification or unit-price for unforeseen conditions.
2. **Moisture.** A failed RH test after award turns a $4/SF epoxy job into a $7+/SF job. Takeoff
   must state the spec's moisture limits and whether mitigation is in, out, or unit-priced.
3. **Edges and small rooms.** Polishing production rates assume open floor. A 40,000 SF warehouse
   and 40,000 SF of classrooms are wildly different jobs. Quantify perimeter LF and room count,
   not just SF.
